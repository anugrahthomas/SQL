ER (Entity-Relationship) diagram:
- visual representation of entities, their attributes, and the relationships between them in a database

Entity: represents the real-world objects or concepts in the database, such as Student, Employee, or Product.

Types of Entities:
- Strong Entity: an entity that can exist independently and has a primary key to uniquely identify its
- Weak Entity: an entity that cannot exist independently and relies on a strong entity for its identification, often having a foreign key that references the primary key of the strong entity.

Attribute: represents the properties or characteristics of an entity, such as name, age, or address.

Types of Attributes:
- Simple Attribute: an attribute that cannot be further divided, such as a person's first name or last name.
- Composite Attribute: an attribute that can be divided into smaller sub-attributes, such as a full name that can be divided into first name and last name.
- Derived Attribute: an attribute that can be derived or calculated from other attributes, such as age derived from date of birth.
- Multivalued Attribute: an attribute that can have multiple values for a single entity, such as a person having multiple phone numbers.

Relationship: represents the association between two or more entities, such as a Student enrolling in a Course.

Types of Relationships:
- One-to-One (1:1): a relationship where one entity is associated with only one other entity, such as a person having one passport.
- One-to-Many (1:N): a relationship where one entity is associated with multiple entities, such as a teacher teaching multiple courses.
- Many-to-One (N:1): a relationship where multiple entities are associated with one entity, such as multiple students belonging to one class.
- Many-to-Many (M:N): a relationship where multiple entities are associated with multiple other entities, such as students enrolling in multiple courses and courses having multiple students.

On Delete and On Update Actions:
- Cascade: when a record in the parent table is deleted or updated, the corresponding records in the child table are also deleted or updated.
- Set Null: when a record in the parent table is deleted or updated, the corresponding foreign key values in the child table are set to NULL.
- Restrict: when a record in the parent table is deleted or updated, the action is restricted if there are corresponding records in the child table, preventing the deletion or update.
-- A parent record cannot be deleted or its referenced key updated if corresponding child records exist. The operation is rejected immediately.
- No Action:
-- Similar to RESTRICT in many databases: the operation is rejected if it would violate the foreign-key constraint. The key distinction is that NO ACTION may allow the constraint violation to be checked at the end of the statement or transaction if the constraint is deferrable, whereas RESTRICT prevents it immediately.
